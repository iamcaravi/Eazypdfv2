Why use Compress PDF?

Compression matters whenever a file's size, not its content, is the problem.

Email attachment limits

Most inboxes cap attachments around 20–25MB. A scanned PDF over that limit needs to shrink before it can be sent at all.

Government and portal uploads

Application portals and government forms frequently cap file size — sometimes as low as 1–2MB per document.

Storage add up fast

A folder of full-resolution scans can quietly use up far more space than the same documents compressed to a reasonable target.

How it works

Upload

Add the PDF you want to shrink. Nothing is sent anywhere at this step.

Set a target size

Enter the exact KB size you need to hit — for an email limit, a form upload cap, or just general storage saving.

Compress

Images and content are re-encoded locally toward your target, entirely in your browser.

Download

Save the smaller file. Your original PDF is untouched.

Frequently asked questions

Can I choose exactly how small the file becomes?

Yes, set a target file size in KB and Compress PDF works toward that size.

Does compression reduce image quality?

Images are re-encoded to hit the target size, so very aggressive targets can visibly reduce image quality.

Is there a file size limit for compressing?

No hard limit is enforced, though very large files depend on your browser's available memory.
